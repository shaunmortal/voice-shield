# Layer Voice Shield; Detectors Alone Won't Hold

As of October 2026 no open-source model and no shipped consumer product reliably detects AI-cloned voices on live cellular calls in Hindi/Hinglish from audio alone, and the two Hugging Face classifiers now in the pipeline are among the weakest options on offer. The best open detectors, all built on a wav2vec2-XLS-R-300M front-end, reach **1–5% EER on the English In-the-Wild set** but **25–33% on Deepfake-Eval-2024** and **40–50% EER, i.e. chance, on the 2026 multilingual ML-ITW benchmark that includes Hindi** ([arXiv 2506.21090](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21090); [arXiv 2603.05852](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05852)), and telephony codecs add at least five more points before any fine-tuning ([arXiv 2504.12423](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12423v2)). The products that actually ship (Google Pixel/Galaxy Scam Detection, LG Uplus ixi-O, Norton) treat the synthetic-voice score as a secondary signal under a transcript-based scam-intent classifier and process controls such as callback verification, and that is the design Voice Shield should copy. The honest 48-hour path is: replace Om-Parab distilhubert and Gustking wav2vec2 with NII's AntiDeepfake Wav2Vec2-Small (95 M, CC BY-NC-SA) and keep Speech-Arena's DF_Arena_100M as the `transformers`-native fallback; build a self-voice clone test set that passes through a real phone-to-Oppo call captured with scrcpy; codec-augment with ffmpeg (AMR-NB, G.711, Opus-NB); fine-tune only a head on the frozen encoder (LoRA as a stretch goal); calibrate per channel; and report EER and TPR@1%FPR per channel and per unseen clone tool. For the Alexa+ track, entrants get no access to Alexa+ devices or the MCP Toolkit, so the demo is your own web page acting as a real Streamable HTTP MCP client against a `FastMCP(json_response=True, stateless_http=True)` server behind a Cloudflare quick tunnel; a Bedrock Nova Micro scam-language classifier plus Guardrails costs cents and qualifies for the AWS Builder mini-challenge. Submission closes Friday 23 Oct 12:00 PDT, which is **Saturday 24 Oct 00:30 IST** ([Devpost Rules](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/rules)).

## Open detectors score 1–5% EER on English benchmarks and ~45% on Hindi in the wild

The field converged in 2025–26 on one architecture: a self-supervised wav2vec2-XLS-R-300M encoder (about 320 M parameters, roughly 1.2 GB in fp32) with a small back-end such as AASIST, SLS, Nes2Net-X or Mamba. In the only unified open evaluation, Speech DF Arena (every system trained on ASVspoof 2019 only), the XLS-R systems cluster at **6.7–7.8% EER on In-the-Wild** (XLSR-Mamba 6.70, XLSR+SLS 7.45, Nes2Net-X 7.75) while raw-waveform AASIST and RawNet2 sit at 43–49%, and proprietary APIs set the ceiling at 1.26% (Whispeak) and 3.94% (Resemble) ([arXiv 2509.02859](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02859)). A 2026 SUPERB-style study of 20 frozen SSL encoders found XLS-R the best front-end at 17.4% mean EER across benchmarks and measured 13.5% average EER across the eleven ASVspoof 5 codec conditions versus 49.8% for FBANK features, which confirms that multilingual SSL pretraining matters far more than the choice of back-end ([arXiv 2603.01482](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01482)).

Two 2025–26 efforts moved the numbers by scaling training data rather than architecture. NII Yamagishi-lab's AntiDeepfake family post-trained SSL encoders on roughly 56,000 h of genuine and 18,000 h of fake speech spanning over 100 languages, including ASVspoof 5, MLAAD (38 languages) and Codecfake; its Wav2Vec2-Large reaches **1.91% EER on In-the-Wild** zero-shot and the 95 M Wav2Vec2-Small reaches 4.23%, yet even the 2.2 B XLS-R variant only manages 25.8% on Deepfake-Eval-2024 ([arXiv 2506.21090](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21090); [HF card](https://huggingface.co/nii-yamagishilab/wav2vec-small-anti-deepfake)). Speech-Arena-2025's DF_Arena_1B, trained on ASVspoof 2019+2024, Codecfake, MLAAD and SpoofCeleb among others, posts 0.91% ITW but 17.25% on ASVspoof 2024 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Speech-Arena-2025/DF_Arena_1B_V_1)); its 500 M and 100 M siblings score 1.76% and 2.21% ITW in an independent controlled study ([arXiv 2603.06164](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.06164)).

The honest counterweight is what happens off-benchmark. Deepfake-Eval-2024 (56.5 h of audio from 88 websites in 52 languages) drove off-the-shelf AASIST, RawNet2 and an SSL model to AUC 0.43–0.58 and EER 43–55%, an average 48% AUC loss, and the best of eight anonymized commercial audio detectors reached only 0.89 accuracy and 0.93 AUC ([arXiv 2503.02857](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02857)). The March 2026 ML-ITW benchmark (14 languages including Hindi, 24,529 segments from 7 platforms) put all eleven evaluated detectors at **40–50% EER**, including XLSR+AASIST at 49.4% and XLSR+SLS at 48.8%; retraining XLSR+AASIST on SpoofCeleb pulled it to 27.65%, which is the realistic shape of what in-domain fine-tuning buys ([arXiv 2603.05852](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.05852)). On Indic data specifically, AASIST trained on English CodecFake scored 40.3% EER on Indic-CodecFake and 26.7–38.7% cross-lingually ([arXiv 2604.19949](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19949)), and the SpeechFake authors report a "noticeable performance drop... particularly for French and Hindi" for English/Chinese-trained models, mitigated only by wav2vec2-XLSR's multilingual pretraining ([arXiv 2507.21463](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.21463)). No open SSL detector publishes a Hindi-specific EER; that number is yours to measure.

The two models currently in the pipeline should be removed rather than ensembled. Om-Parab/distilhubert-finetuned-audio-deepfake-in-the-wild is a 23.7 M-parameter auto-generated card with no base model, dataset, metrics, sample rate or license listed ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Om-Parab/distilhubert-finetuned-audio-deepfake-in-the-wild)), and no DistilHuBERT-class encoder appears in the competitive tier of any 2025–26 benchmark. Gustking/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-deepfake-audio-classification is Apache-2.0 and sits on the right backbone, but it was fine-tuned through an intermediate gender-recognition model with unspecified data and reports an in-domain ASVspoof 2019 EER of 4.01% ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Gustking/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-deepfake-audio-classification)), roughly twenty times worse than XLSR+SLS on that set, with no out-of-domain evidence at all. The nearest community cousin that was independently tested, MelodyMachine/Deepfake-audio-detection-V2, **missed 90.9% of modern fakes** at 53% accuracy ([Podonos](https://www.podonos.com/blog/audio-deepfake-detection-benchmark)), and nothing suggests the two current models behave differently on Hindi phone audio. Averaging two uncalibrated weak scores does not make a strong one; it makes an uncalibrated weak one with a longer README.

## Put 10–20% EER in the README, not 95% accuracy

The telephony penalty is well measured at 16 kHz and unmeasured at 8 kHz AMR-NB, which is what most Indian cellular calls carry. ASVspoof 5's evaluation set passed audio through MP3, Opus, AMR, Speex, Encodec and a simulated mobile-to-PSTN path; in the closed condition neural Encodec was hardest, followed by narrowband 8 kHz DSP codecs, then 16 kHz codecs, and the organizers conclude that top systems "consistently struggle with generalization" under neural compression and out-of-domain data ([arXiv 2601.03944](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03944v1)). A 2025 benchmark that simulated AMR-WB, EVS, Opus, Speex-WB and SILK with 0–20% packet loss found clean-trained baselines lose an average **5.30 EER points** from clean to codec, while codec-plus-packet-loss augmented training shrank the gap to 0.003 ([arXiv 2504.12423](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12423v2)). SAFE 2025 found speech codecs, "phone-audio codec" processing and chained MP3/AAC "degraded performance significantly", with over-the-air re-recording the single most damaging condition ([arXiv 2510.03387](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03387v2)). Reality Defender's own ASVspoof 5 entry saw minDCF three to five times higher on two codec conditions despite a 5.56% overall EER ([arXiv 2410.07379](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.07379)). The mechanism is clear: AMR-NB constrains bandwidth to roughly 300–3400 Hz ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/3939840)), and CELP codecs "over-quantize or eliminate components that deviate from expected speech norms", suppressing exactly the artifacts detectors learned ([arXiv 2508.01467](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.01467v1)). Deepfake-Eval-2024 adds three failure modes that describe every Indian phone call: silent segments (accuracy 35 points worse), background music (false-negative rate +26 points) and non-English audio (−7 points) ([arXiv 2503.02857](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02857)).

No paper reports per-codec EER for AMR-NB or EVS-NB for any open SSL detector, and Podonos's 8 kHz telephony leaderboard (G.711, AMR-NB 12.2 kbps, G.729, GSM-FR) still has no entries ([Podonos](https://www.podonos.com/blog/audio-deepfake-detection-benchmark)). That gap is your opportunity: a per-codec table measured on a real Indian VoLTE call would be the first of its kind and a more credible README than any borrowed figure. Until you measure it, the defensible prior is that a Tier-1 model lands at **10–20% EER on real Indian cellular audio before fine-tuning**, obtained by stacking the +5-point 16 kHz codec shift, ASVspoof 5's finding that 8 kHz is harder still, and the 15–25-point cross-lingual penalty English-trained models show on Indic fakes. Vendor claims do not change this: Hiya advertises "over 99% accuracy" with no metric definition or false-positive rate ([Hiya AI Voice](https://www.hiya.com/products/ai-voice)), Pindrop resellers quote 99% with under 1% false positives and 90% on unseen fakes ([SoftwareOne](https://platform.softwareone.com/product/pindrop-pulse/PCP-8653-3864)), and the only independent cross-vendor evaluation caps the best commercial detector at 0.89 accuracy on social-media audio before any telephony degradation ([arXiv 2503.02857](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02857)).

Real-time voice conversion needs its own caveat line. RVC was chosen in the DEEP-VOICE work precisely for its ability "to quickly convert short speech samples" in real time ([arXiv 2308.12734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.12734)); RVCBench (Feb 2026) benchmarks 18 modern VC models and warns that detector-facing separability is not robustness ([arXiv 2602.00443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00443)); and a Jan 2026 streaming detector works on 1-second windows but reports no cross-model generalization ([arXiv 2601.04227](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04227)). No shipped product publishes VC-versus-TTS detection rates. Live VC preserves the scammer's own prosody, breaths and room, removing the cues that content classifiers and humans rely on; combined with codec loss this is plausibly the hardest shipped case. The README should say you tested it (Seed-VC offline, see the plan) rather than claim coverage.

Latency deserves the same honesty. The only CPU measurement found puts a full XLS-R-300M forward pass on a 5 s clip at about 9 s on a single CPU core, and the authors conclude the full model "cannot be used for real-time processing on the single CPU core" ([arXiv 2606.30780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30780)). Extrapolating to all M1 performance cores or MPS gives a real-time factor around 0.3–0.6 for a 320 M model, enough for a 4 s hop with 2 s overlap; the 95 M Wav2Vec2-Small roughly triples that headroom. Memory is not the constraint: 320 M fp32 is about 1.3 GB, two such models fit in 8 GB, a 1 B model does not comfortably. Measure and report your own numbers; none of the ranked models has a published M1 figure.

## Shipped products trust transcripts and callbacks; the voice score is a tiebreaker

Every consumer feature marketed as real-time scam-call detection is content-, metadata- or caller-ID-based, not voice-artifact-based. Google describes Pixel Scam Detection as "AI models processed on-device to analyze conversations in real-time", with a caller pressing for gift-card payment as the example trigger; the announcement never mentions synthetic audio, uses Gemini Nano on Pixel 9+, publishes no detection or false-positive rate, and ships off by default with audio processed ephemerally ([Google Security Blog](https://blog.google/security/new-ai-powered-scam-detection-features/)). The same Google model reached Galaxy S26 in February 2026, US English only, with Samsung saying One UI 9 will bring it to Japan, Canada and India ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Samsung-One-UI-9-Security-features-you-must-turn-on-yourself.1415703.0.html)). Apple's iOS 26 Call Screening asks unknown callers for a name and reason and shows a transcript; Hiya's own assessment is that "it doesn't block or evaluate spam or scam calls" ([Hiya blog](https://blog.hiya.com/what-you-need-to-know-about-apples-ios-26-call-screening)). In India, DoT's spoofed-call system blocked about 1.35 crore international calls presenting Indian CLI in its first 24 hours and then mandated an "International Call" label when scammers shifted to foreign numbers ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/12/223-dot-blocked-90-percent-spoofed-calls-two-months/)), and Airtel's network filter flagged 8 billion calls in two months using frequency, duration and pattern features ([getcoai](https://getcoai.com/news/indian-telco-airtel-says-its-blocked-8-billion-spam-calls-using-ai-in-just-2-months)). No Indian telco runs voice-deepfake detection on calls.

The handful of systems that do run artifact detection on live telephony all wrap it in something else. LG Uplus ixi-O, the only confirmed fully on-device real-time anti-deepvoice feature on a cellular dialer, is a hybrid pipeline of VAD, speech-to-text, a lightweight quantized LLM vishing classifier trained on Korean police case data, and an "Anti-DeepVoice" module, with audio never leaving the device; it claims 95% accuracy on an undefined metric and roughly 5,500 detections a month ([GSMA case study](https://www.gsma.com/solutions-and-impact/technologies/security/scams/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Case-Study_Real-time-On-device-Vishing-Detection-and-Anti-DeepVoice-Protection-v2.pdf)). Norton's Deepfake Protection explicitly fuses "is the audio AI-generated" with "does the message appear aimed at scamming", in English only ([Norton](https://us.norton.com/blog/online-scams/npu-scam-detection)). Hiya's app flags "a person, a recording, or an AI-generated voice" alongside scam-language analysis ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/01/28/hiya-ai-phone/)); Truecaller's 2024 AI Call Scanner was on-demand, US/Android/English only, Hindi "in progress", and launched with no accuracy figures and "a lack of transparency specifying exactly how the scanner has been trained" ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2024/05/223-truecaller-ai-voice-scanner-spam-call/)). The research matches the product pattern: across seven LLMs on the first aligned audio-transcript scam dataset, transcript inputs consistently beat raw-audio inputs and ASR transcripts performed as well as human-corrected ones ([arXiv 2606.24523](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24523)); a fine-tuned Llama3-8B on transcripts matches a GPT-4 vishing detector, with an adversarial set of benign transcripts containing scam vocabulary as the hard false-positive test ([arXiv 2506.06180](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06180v1)); and a 2025 text-plus-audio fusion paper motivates its audio branch precisely because text-only systems misclassify legitimate calls that mention banks and money ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/20/11170/xml)).

Official guidance is unanimous that the decisive countermeasure is human and procedural. The FBI's December 2024 PSA says to "create a secret word or phrase with your family" and to "verify the identity of the person calling you by hanging up the phone" and calling a number you looked up yourself ([FBI IC3](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA241203)); the FTC says "don't trust the voice", call the person back on a known number, and treat requests to wire money, send crypto or buy gift cards as the scam signature ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2023/03/scammers-use-ai-enhance-their-family-emergency-schemes)); Tamil Nadu Police tell citizens to contact the supposed caller "through a known, verified number" before acting ([DT Next](https://www.pressreader.com/india/dt-next/20240428/281621015402986)); and the RBI's standing advice is never to share OTP, PIN or card details "even with bank officials, however genuine they might sound" ([RBI](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx?prid=53185)). The scale justifies the design: India's reported cyber-fraud losses were at least ₹22,495 crore on 2.81 million complaints in 2025 ([Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/housing-society-problems-and-solutions-occupancy-rights-leakage-disputes-and-agm-decisions/article/fraud-alert-india-is-losing-over-22000-crore-a-year-in-cyber-scams-and-the-worst-is-yet-to-come/80612.html)), and digital-arrest complaints peaked at 123,672 (₹1,935 crore) in 2024 ([Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/supreme-court-directs-measures-to-curb-digital-arrest-scams)); no agency counts voice-clone cases separately, so the voice score is a feature inside a scam problem, not the problem itself.

### The four layers and the fusion rule

Layer 0 is caller metadata, computed before any audio is heard: unknown number claiming to be family, a foreign prefix or the DoT "International Call" label, a number with no prior call history, or a CLI that differs from the saved contact. These are the signals India's own defenses run on, they cost nothing, and they cannot be defeated by a better TTS model. Layer 1 is the acoustic clone score from the replaced detector, computed on 4 s windows and aggregated to a call-level score with the per-channel calibration described below. Layer 2 is conversation content: a rolling transcript (on-device Whisper or Transcribe streaming `hi-IN`/`en-IN`) classified by an LLM into a structured verdict such as `{tactic, urgency, secrecy, payment_request, impersonation, confidence}`, with OTP, wire, crypto, gift-card, "keep this secret" and "don't hang up" as the explicit red-flag vocabulary the FTC and RBI name. Layer 3 is human verification: the tool never asks the user to decide from a probability, it asks the family secret-word question, offers a one-tap callback to the contact's known number, and routes a confirmed scam to 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, which is what every police advisory recommends.

The fusion rule should be ordered, not averaged: content and metadata gate, audio boosts, humans decide. A high content score with any metadata anomaly triggers the alert regardless of the voice score; a high voice score alone only raises the interface to "unverified caller, ask the secret word" and never auto-blocks, because a ~10% false-positive rate on a feature that fires on every call is unusable and is almost certainly why Google chose content classification over per-call voice liveness. This mirrors Norton and LG Uplus, both of which treat the artifact score as a secondary feature that gates or boosts a content score. It also matches the legal shape of the problem: Indian voice data is personal data under the DPDP Act and recording it is processing ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/10/22/covert-voice-recording-matrimonial-disputes-privacy-infringement-dpdpa/)), so keep inference on-device, never persist or upload the remote party's audio, and position the processing under the personal-or-domestic-purpose exemption in Section 3(c)(i) ([dpdpa.com](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-1/section3.html)); whether that exemption covers a consumer app analyzing the remote party in real time is unsettled, so say so in the README and send only the transcript, never audio, to Bedrock.

Hours 0–3 set up a Python 3.9 virtualenv for AntiDeepfake (fairseq 0.12.2 plus safetensors per the model card ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/nii-yamagishilab/wav2vec-small-anti-deepfake))), score twenty clips you already have, and log seconds per 4 s window on CPU and MPS. The fallback loads in one line: `transformers.pipeline("antispoofing", model="Speech-Arena-2025/DF_Arena_100M_V_1", trust_remote_code=True)` ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/Speech-Arena-2025/DF_Arena_1B_V_1)); if you land there, check the repository's LICENSE text before relying on it, because the org page omits it for the 100 M and 500 M variants.

Hours 3–10 build the clone set from one teammate's consented voice. Record 10–15 minutes of that person reading phone-style scripts in Hindi, Hinglish and English (family emergency, bank OTP, "digital arrest", plus neutral chat as the benign control); this is also your bona fide set. Clone with F5-TTS via the MLX port, which targets Apple Silicon ([HF](https://huggingface.co/aufklarer/F5TTS-v1-Base-MLX-fp16)), using the Hinglish fine-tune built on SPRINGLab's F5-Hindi-24KHz (151 M, 3–10 s reference) ([HF](https://huggingface.co/SwarajSolanke-turtle/F5-TTS-Hinglish)); F5-TTS weights are CC-BY-NC-4.0, which is fine for a test set. Second local family: Chatterbox Multilingual (about 500 M, Hindi included, with a Hindi-specific fine-tune `ResembleAI/Chatterbox-Multilingual-hi`), remembering the sample code defaults to `device="cuda"` and must be switched to `mps` or `cpu` ([HF README](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox/blob/main/README.md)). Third family, and the one that represents the live-conversion threat: Seed-VC, zero-shot from a 1–30 s reference, which lists Apple Silicon support and quotes about 400 ms end-to-end delay; run it offline, file-to-file, converting the second teammate's speech into the first's voice, because real-time quality on an M1 with 8 GB will be marginal and the acoustic artifacts are the same either way ([Seed-VC README](https://gittrend.io/repo/Plachtaa/seed-vc)). Fourth and fifth families come from commercial free tiers (ElevenLabs about 10,000 credits a month, Fish Audio about 8,000) whose terms vary by review site and must be confirmed on the vendor pages ([dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/best-ai-voice-cloning-tools)). Run exactly one local model at a time; 8 GB is the binding constraint. Five families gives you a clean seen/unseen split later.

Hours 10–14 are the part no borrowed benchmark can replace. Stock Android lets an app access only its own microphone stream and blocks silent splicing into another app's audio ([voxbooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/voice-changer-for-android)), so the legitimate and, as it turns out, more realistic route is acoustic: place a call between two team phones, play each clip through the laptop speaker held next to the sending phone, and capture on the Oppo with your already-verified scrcpy voice-call path. The "It's All in the Presentation" work shows detectors fail when training ignores how audio arrives through the channel, explicitly validates loudspeaker playback as a presentation method, and reports a 39% accuracy gain in lab setups and 57% on a real-world benchmark from modelling it ([arXiv 2509.26471](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.26471)); Resemble's ReplayDF confirms the speaker-to-microphone path strips artifacts detectors rely on ([Resemble](https://www.resemble.ai/resources/replay-attacks-against-audio-deepfake-detection)). Play the bona fide recordings and some live human speech through the same path so the real-call condition has both classes. Before the batch, send a known clip and inspect its spectrogram: a 3.4 kHz ceiling means AMR-NB, 7 kHz means AMR-WB/EVS-WB, and that decides which offline codec to treat as "matched".

Hours 14–18 generate the offline channel conditions. The AMR-NB encoder in ffmpeg refuses anything but 8 kHz mono ([FFmpeg source](https://ffmpeg.org/doxygen/8.0/libopencore-amr_8c_source.html)), so the chain is band-limit and resample first, then round-trip through each codec:

```bash
ffmpeg -i in.wav -af "highpass=f=300,lowpass=f=3400" -ar 8000 -ac 1 band.wav
ffmpeg -i band.wav -c:a libopencore_amrnb -b:a 12.2k amr.amr && ffmpeg -i amr.amr -ar 16000 amr_nb.wav
ffmpeg -i band.wav -c:a pcm_mulaw g711.wav && ffmpeg -i g711.wav -ar 16000 g711_16k.wav
ffmpeg -i band.wav -c:a libopus -b:a 12k -application voip opus.ogg && ffmpeg -i opus.ogg -ar 16000 opus_nb.wav
```

Produce six conditions per clip (clean, band-limited, AMR-NB at 7.4 and 12.2 kbps, G.711 µ-law, Opus-NB, real-call), add 1–10% simulated packet loss on a subset, and write a manifest with speaker, tool, condition, label and split. Verify the exact flags against your ffmpeg build; AMR-WB needs `libvo_amrwbenc`, which is not in every build. If you want a public held-out family beyond your own clones, pull a sampled Hindi subset of IndicSynth, the ACL 2025 outstanding-paper corpus of over 4,000 h of synthetic speech in 12 Indian languages with a Hindi loader example on the HF mirror ([ACL](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1070); [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/vdivyasharma/IndicSynth)), and pass it through the same chain; its license is not stated in the sources, so cite it as evaluation-only.

Hours 18–28 adapt the detector without ever unfreezing the encoder on 8 GB. The must-do is a head: run the frozen encoder over every training file, pool the embeddings (or take the weighted-sum of layers if the model exposes them), and fit a logistic regression or two-layer MLP on the three seen tool families across all channel conditions; that is minutes of compute and the same shape as the "freeze wav2vec 2.0, swap the back-end" recipe that reached 7.6% EER on ASVspoof 5 ([arXiv 2507.11777](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11777v1)). The stretch is a single rank-8 LoRA inside the Wav2Vec2-Small encoder, which the September 2025 study found beats full fine-tuning out of domain (8.22% average EER versus over 19% for full fine-tuning on ASVspoof 5) ([arXiv 2509.13878](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13878v1)); on 95 M parameters this fits in memory on MPS, on 300 M it is marginal, and either way cap it at two hours. Add RawBoost during training, which synthesizes telephony-like noise and distortion from the waveform itself with no external data and gave a 27% relative gain on the PSTN/VoIP-transmitted ASVspoof 2021 LA set ([RawBoost repo](https://github.com/TakHemlata/RawBoost-antispoofing)). The published evidence says this augmentation, not architecture, is what closes the codec gap ([arXiv 2504.12423](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12423v2)).

Hours 28–32 produce the numbers. Fit a temperature or Platt scaler per channel condition on a held-out calibration split; the 2026 "When EER Hides Deployment Failure" audit shows a threshold tuned on one channel does not transfer to another and that EER alone conceals this ([arXiv 2606.21584](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.21584)). Then compute, per channel and per clone tool, EER (the point where false-accept equals false-reject on the ROC) and TPR@1%FPR (the fraction of fakes caught at the threshold that flags 1% of genuine clips), with bootstrap confidence intervals and the sample size stated; accuracy is the wrong metric for a rare-event problem. Report three headline numbers: seen-tool EER on offline codecs (optimistic, and say so), unseen-tool EER on the two held-out families, and the real-call-recorded condition, which is the deployment number. Treat the ML-ITW and Deepfake-Eval-2024 results as the reason your unseen-tool number is the honest one, and consider running the AUDDT toolkit over the before-and-after checkpoints to show a standard cross-dataset sanity check ([arXiv 2509.21597](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21597)). A few hundred clips per condition is a reasonable hackathon floor, but it is a reasoned estimate rather than a published minimum, so phrase the confidence accordingly.

## Alexa+ entrants get no device, so the web MCP client is the demo

The hackathon FAQ is explicit: "Amazon's Alexa+ Add-on developer tools... are in preview and available to select partners only - there is currently no way for hackathon participants to apply for or gain access", and the accepted path is to "build a self-hosted MCP server (or Agent Skill) per the Official Rules, and demo it via your own web-based simulator or front end" ([Devpost FAQs](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/details/faqs)). The same FAQ confirms that a web page acting as a real MCP client, "sending initialize, tools-list, and tools-call requests over Streamable HTTP", satisfies the requirement, that a locally runnable repo plus the video is enough because judges can build and test it themselves, and that the server does not need to stay running after submission. The rules add the runtime-hook requirement for the Alexa+ track: the repository must show the MCP library imported and actually called, not just named in the README, and the required spec is 2025-11-25 over Streamable HTTP ([Devpost Rules](https://amazonappdev2026.devpost.com/rules)). "Agent Skill" in this context means the open agentskills.io SKILL.md format the Resources tab links to through the MCP Apps extension repo ([MCP Apps API](https://apps.extensions.modelcontextprotocol.io/api/)), not an Alexa Skills Kit skill; shipping a `SKILL.md` that teaches a coding agent how to call Voice Shield's tools is a cheap way to tick the "Agent Skills" box judges list under creative. Alexa+ itself reached India in Early Access on 16 September 2026 speaking English, Hindi and Hinglish ([aboutamazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/alexa-plus-international-launch)), which makes a Hindi/Hinglish scam shield on-theme even though that launch gives you consumer, not developer, access.

Build the server to Amazon's published MCP Toolkit requirements anyway, because it costs nothing and is the best possible Friction Log entry ("designed to the published checklist; blocked only by preview gating"). Those requirements are Streamable HTTP only with legacy HTTP+SSE migrated, a public HTTPS URL where "a tunnel such as cloudflared works" for development, round-trip under 500 ms, and either no auth or OAuth 2.1 authorization-code with PKCE S256 and static client registration ([Alexa+ MCP QuickStart](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/alexaplus/add-ons/mcp-toolkit-quickstart)). Alexa+'s real client sends `initialize` with `protocolVersion` `2025-03-26` even though the toolkit claims 2025-11-25 support, has "no explicit MCP session identifier", and expects tool results to carry `structuredContent` plus a text block with the same JSON, optionally with `_meta.ui` for an MCP App surface ([Alexa+ MCP Client and App Lifecycle](https://developer.amazon.com/docs/alexaplus/add-ons/mcp-toolkit-client-lifecycle)). A stateless server therefore matches Alexa+'s own model, and your simulator becomes credibly Alexa-shaped if it mimics that handshake and renders the `structuredContent` as a card.

The tunnel decision is forced by one line in Cloudflare's documentation: "Quick Tunnels do not support Server-Sent Events (SSE)", alongside a 200 in-flight request cap and a hostname that changes per run ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-one/networks/connectors/cloudflare-tunnel/do-more-with-tunnels/trycloudflare/)). The 2025-11-25 transport lets a server answer a POST with plain `application/json` and return 405 on GET, and requires Origin validation with 403 on failure ([MCP spec](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/basic/transports)), so construct the server in JSON-response, stateless mode and you never emit SSE. The second trap is the Python SDK's DNS-rebinding guard, which returns `421 Invalid Host header` when the tunnel hostname is absent from `allowed_hosts`; setting a non-localhost `host=` does not add it, and the fix is to pass `transport_security` with the hostname listed bare and with `:*` ([MCP Python SDK](https://py.sdk.modelcontextprotocol.io/v2/api/mcp/server/transport_security/)).

```python
from mcp.server.fastmcp import FastMCP
from mcp.server.transport_security import TransportSecuritySettings
mcp = FastMCP("voice-shield", json_response=True, stateless_http=True,
    transport_security=TransportSecuritySettings(
        enable_dns_rebinding_protection=True,
        allowed_hosts=["localhost:*", "127.0.0.1:*",
                       "<rand>.trycloudflare.com", "<rand>.trycloudflare.com:*"]))
app = mcp.streamable_http_app()   # uvicorn app --port 8000 ; endpoint /mcp
```

Confirm the argument names against the installed `mcp` 2.x before relying on them. Because the quick-tunnel hostname is random per run, read it from `cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:8000` and inject it via an environment variable, or disable rebinding protection for the demo only and document that as a deliberate corner cut. ngrok's free tier (three endpoints, 1 GB, 20k requests) works but puts an interstitial on browser requests ([ngrok pricing](https://ngrok.com/pricing)), which your browser-based MCP client will hit; Cloudflare is simpler. The web client itself is four POSTs to `/mcp` with `Content-Type: application/json`, `Accept: application/json, text/event-stream` and `MCP-Protocol-Version: 2025-11-25`: `initialize`, `notifications/initialized` (expect 202), `tools/list`, then `tools/call` on tools such as `analyze_call_audio`, `check_caller`, `classify_transcript` and `verify_callback`, with the result rendered as an Alexa-style card and spoken reply. Measure the round-trip and put it next to Amazon's 500 ms guidance; a 4 s window through a 95 M encoder on M1 should clear it, a 320 M encoder may not, and that comparison belongs in the Product Feedback.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the project: Voice Shield's value is not a better audio classifier but an honest, layered decision system in which the audio score is the least reliable input and the only one an attacker can optimize against. The novel contribution a two-person team can actually make in 48 hours is empirical rather than architectural: the first per-codec, per-clone-tool EER and TPR@1%FPR table measured on a real Indian cellular call in Hindi/Hinglish, with a seen/unseen split and a labelled real-time VC row, published next to a README that quotes 10–20% rather than 99%. That table is more defensible to a judge, more useful to the field, and harder to produce than any model swap.

The forward implication is that the "open versus proprietary" gap on In-the-Wild audio is mostly a data gap that post-training closed, while the "benchmark versus phone call" gap is a channel and language gap nobody has published numbers for; the codec-augmentation literature says it is cheap to close for seen generators and nothing says it closes for unseen ones. Design the MCP tools so the content and callback layers carry the verdict, keep the detector swappable behind a calibrated score, and the system stays credible when the next clone tool ships the week after the hackathon.
